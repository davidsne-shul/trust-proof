What the page shows

Four things a résumé cannot say about you.

Days of record

How far back the evidence runs. Time is the one thing that cannot be generated after the fact.

Comebacks

Every stretch you were away — and the day you came back. Streak counters punish the pause. This one counts the return.

Languages, dated

Not a list. A timeline of when each one entered your work — breadth that accumulated month by month.

Long projects

The things you were still pushing to ninety days after you started them.

No score. No ranking. No comparison to anyone else. Every number on the page says, in one line, exactly how it was counted.

The second layer

Not everything worth showing lives in a repository.

A designer, a writer, someone who works with their hands — none of them have a commit history, and all of them have a record. So you can add to your page yourself: a photo, a link, one line about what it was.

Dated the moment it arrives

You cannot place an item in the past. Twelve things added the night before an interview show as twelve things from one day — nothing is blocked, and nothing needs to be said about it. It simply reads as what it is.

Kept apart from the record

What you add sits beside what was counted, never inside it. No combined number, no total, nothing that rewards whoever uploads most. Two layers, and the reader decides.
